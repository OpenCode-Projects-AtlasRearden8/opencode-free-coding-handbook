Free AI Coding with OpenCode

A beginner's manual for coding with AI at zero cost

A plain-English, beginner-first manual for installing and using OpenCode — the open-source AI coding agent — with free models, real safety rails, Git as your undo button, and honest notes on free-tier limits. No paid accounts required anywhere in this book.

    ~5 min read

  • Chapter 1 — What Is OpenCode?

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 2 — Installing OpenCode

    Installation sounds scary if you have never done "developer stuff" before. Good news: it is usually one command, and this chapter shows every option with full copy-paste instructions. Ten minutes from now you will have the tool running.

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 3 — Picking Free Models

    OpenCode is the tool; the model is the engine. This chapter gets you an engine without spending money, and explains the honest trade-offs you are making.

    ~7 min read

  • Chapter 4 — Permissions and Safety

    Here is the sentence that makes this chapter matter: an agent that can edit files and run commands is powerful, and power without guardrails is how beginners get hurt. OpenCode ships with sensible guardrails. This chapter shows you how they work, how to tune them, and how to develop habits that make AI coding safe even on a free model.

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 5 — Writing Good Task Prompts

    Bad prompts are the single biggest cause of "the AI is useless" experiences — and the fix costs nothing. This chapter gives you a formula, worked examples, and the little OpenCode mechanics (@, images, Plan mode) that turn decent prompts into great ones.

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 6 — Git Basics (Your Safety Net)

    Git is the one tool in this book that has nothing directly to do with AI — and is the reason AI coding is safe for beginners. Git records snapshots of your project, called commits, so that any mistake — yours or the AI's — can be reversed with one command. Everything else in this book quietly assumes you know this chapter.

    ~5 min read

  • Chapter 7 — Reviewing AI Changes

    If prompts are how you direct the AI, review is how you keep your project yours. Skipping review is how people end up with folders they no longer understand. This chapter gives you a light, repeatable review routine — light enough that you'll actually do it every time.

    ~5 min read

  • Chapter 8 — Limits of Free Tiers (and Working Within Them)

    Free is a real option, not a broken version of paid. But free has edges, and beginners exhaust good free setups for lack of knowing the edges' shapes. This chapter maps the limits so you can plan around them instead of being surprised by them.

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 9 — Troubleshooting

    Everything breaks sometimes — terminals, keys, models, permissions — and the fix is almost always in the top ten possibilities. This chapter is a giant symptom → cause → fix table plus a few "get unstuck manually" walkthroughs. Keep it open when something misbehaves; skim it on a good day so it's familiar on a bad one.

    ~6 min read

  • Chapter 10 — Appendix: The Checklists and Glossary

    This is the shortest chapter and the one you'll use most. It compresses the whole book into routines you can run without re-reading anything. Print these, or keep this chapter open as a tab.

    ~4 min read
